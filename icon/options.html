Prayer Lock · app icon options

Five pure-vector directions in the Peacock palette (teal #0D3B42/#124A52, gold #E9B949, cream #F4EBD9). Each is drawn on the 108dp adaptive canvas with the mark inside the 66dp safe zone. The top two from the independent reviewer are first and outlined in gold. Nothing has shipped: the app launcher and store/icon-512.png are unchanged. Exports are in design/icon/png/.

Reviewer #1 · 26/30

A. Mala Lock

Mala ring as the lock body; the gold meru bead carries the shackle

The app in one mark: your mala is the lock, and the meru bead (where every round of jap ends) is where it opens.

Silhouette check: Pass. Reads as a padlock: a dotted ring under a thick arch. No vertical taper, all closed round forms.

The only option where the mala itself is the lock, so the concept is visible at a glance and no other bhakti app looks like it. Fix: at 48px the 16 tight beads read as a gear or cog, so use about 10-12 larger beads with a little space between them, clean up where the shackle meets the beads, and make the centre dot a proper keyhole shape.

Reviewer #2 · 23/30

D. Mandir Niche

Gold puja-shelf arch with a hanging mala

The home shrine (the alcove every Hindu home keeps) with a mala hung in it: a quiet place to pause, not a gadget.

Silhouette check: Pass, with a caution. A plain semicircular arch (no finial knob) on a shelf with a U of beads. The arch is the thinnest outline here, and the beads fuse into a U at 48px.

A respectful, original mandir niche with a mala, but it does not say 'lock'. At 48px it reads as a doorway, a tombstone or a necklace on display, and the beads bunch into a blob where they meet the meru. Fix: space the beads out at the bottom, remove the thin inner arch line that disappears when small, and add a small lock hint (for example the meru bead shaped as a keyhole).

B. Akhand Diya

Horizontal diya, round flame orb (no teardrop)

A low, wide diya with a still, round light: the moment of calm after the scroll stops, with no flame silhouette at all.

Silhouette check: Pass. A wide bowl and a free-floating disc. Horizontal mass, and the light is a circle, not a teardrop.

Gold on teal looks warm, but a diya with no flame reads as a gravy boat or a lamp that has gone out, and the orb inside its grey ring reads as a sun or an eyeball. Diya icons are also everywhere in this category. Fix: drop it, or give it a real flame shape (horizontal or squat, not a tapered vertical) and take out the muddy grey ring.

C. Om Lock

ॐ cut out of a padlock

Most literal: a gold padlock whose keyhole is ॐ, so the only way in is the name.

Silhouette check: Pass. A solid padlock block; the ॐ shows as a cut-out. Its tail curls sideways inside the body, not as a free vertical stroke.

The clearest 'lock' at small sizes, but it is the stock padlock icon with a font Om on it, which is template-looking and blends into the many Om apps. Some users may also dislike seeing Om locked up. Fix: if it stays, draw the Om by hand with even stroke weights, but it makes a better fallback than a lead option.

E. Shankh

Own idea: the conch that opens every puja, lying horizontal

The shankh is blown to begin worship and silence the room; here it is the call that interrupts the scroll. Rarely used by devotional apps.

Silhouette check: Pass, but weakest. A horizontal spindle with stacked whorls; nothing vertical or tapered upward. At 48px it can read as a seed pod or fish rather than a shankh.

An unusual motif that avoids the Om and diya cliches, but lying on its side the conch reads as a fish, an egg, a beetle or a garlic bulb, and the gold band looks like yolk. The tapered spire with a rounded body sits close to the founder's no-tadpole rule even though it is horizontal. Fix: show the conch upright at three-quarter view with a clear spiral and opening, and use more gold so cream does not dominate the teal.
